## 1. What we set out to answer

> The city has two records of its own streets that tell different stories about street conditions.
> 
> 
> **311 Data: 224,643** complaints filed by the public.
> 
> **Street Conditions App: pilot where 6,055** street photos were taken by staff.
> 
> Three questions: 
> 
> 1. Can we generate valid baseline and over-time estimates of street conditions that are comparable across time and space?
> 2. Does the Street Conditions App data tell us anything new compared to 311?
> 3. Can these data inform policy and operational decisions?

---

## 2. Street Conditions App

> From Jan 1 - Jun 30th 2026, staff walked routes and photographed what they saw. An AI scored every photo on 12 categories, 0–3.
> 
> 
> **6,055 photos → 2,239 blocks** (H3 hexagons, ~76 m across, roughly one block face), over **19 weeks**.

> Coverage concentrated in SOMA and the Tenderloin.

## 3. 311

> **224,643** mapped complaints over the same six months, across **6,054 blocks**, continuous
every single day.
> 
> 
> 311 has a number of limitations:
> 
> - **It has no denominator:** 50 cases may mean a bad block or a well-documented one
> - **It cannot record a clean street:** silence isn’t evidence
> - **A complaint isn't a confirmed problem:** **15.3%** of tickets closed as *No Work Needed*
> - **No accountability trail**: No way to validate whether closed tickets equates to addressed work

---

## 4. Insight 1 — Complaint-based resourcing leads to some neighborhoods being consistently underserved

An example of how this plays out:

> **While Bayview–Hunters Point and the Mission have effectively the same street conditions., the Mission files 4.5× the complaints.**
> 

|  | Observations | Found an issue | Severity | **311 cases** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Bayview Hunters Point** | 221 | **71%** | **0.99** | **10,200** |
| Mission | 352 | 70% | 0.85 | **45,479** |

> Bayview is marginally *worse* on both measures the app collects. It generates less than a
quarter of the complaints. **Any budget that follows complaint volume follows civic voice, not street condition.**
> 
> 
> 

**Visual** Their existing bar-pair card, Bayview and Mission. Four rows: observations, share
finding an issue, severity, 311 cases. The first three near-identical bars, the fourth
catastrophically lopsided. That visual asymmetry *is* the argument.

## 5. Insight 2 — A denominator changes our understanding of street conditions

**311 data counts complaints. App data counts X things seen on X visits. Both are proxies for real conditions, but app data may be a more reliable proxy.**

> The app can say: **waste was present on 4 of 8 visits to 5th Street (50% of the time).**
> 
> 
> 311 can only say: 50 cases. Is that a bad block, or a well-documented one? 
> 
> Watch what it does to the Mission. Loudest neighbourhood in the city — an open 311 case
> **96% of days**, 45,479 cases. But when staff actually looked, severity came in at **0.85 out of 3**,
> mid-pack. **Loud, but not the worst.**
> 

**Visual** Left: one hex with 8 tick marks, 4 filled — a rate. Right: "45,479" with no
denominator underneath it, and a question mark where the rate would be.

## 6. Insight 3 — A lot of streets are clean!

> **Of 3,878 unique sightings, 1,511 — 39% — found nothing wrong at all.**
> 
> 
> 311 can never give this level of insight. A block with no complaints is indistinguishable from a block nobody called about. **Documenting general street conditions can establish that a street is clean in a way that documenting problems cannot.**
> 

| Where staff looked | Clean observations |
| --- | --- |
| Oceanview/Merced/Ingleside | **68%** |
| West of Twin Peaks | **67%** |
| Sunset/Parkside | **52%** |
| … |  |
| Tenderloin | 24% |
| Nob Hill | **18%** |

Why this matters:

- City teams deserve credit where credit is due
- Media narratives shouldn’t only be steered by what’s wrong

## 7. The Street Conditions App delivers real value

1. **Can we generate valid baseline and overtime estimates of street conditions that are comparable across time and space?**
    
    Baseline data: **Yes!** 
    
    Caveats: highly variable blocks might need special data collection strategies
    
    Over time data: Yes!
    
    Caveats: coverage depends on how large-scale we’re deploying the app
    
2. **Does the Street Conditions App data tell us anything new compared to 311?**
    - **There are neighborhoods that are currently being underserved**
    - **Adds a denominator that’s a closer proxy for real conditions.** Rates, not just counts
    - **Proves streets are clean,** something no complaint system can do
3. **Can these data inform policy and operational decisions?**
    - **Yes,** see next slide

## 8. This data can inform policy and operational decisions

**SCA data can tell us:**

- **Reveal hidden inequities:** spot areas where 311 reports understate real need, making the case for renewed investment.
- **Establish ambient baselines:** build the evidence base for strategic resource allocation and public-facing investment cases.
- **Surface long-range trends:** track outcomes over time to evaluate past decisions and measure whether interventions actually work.

## 9. What are possible applications of this data?

- **Give DPH/RRT teams better baselines and trends**: more confident strategic deployments - based on real conditions, not ticket counts; get credit for real improvements (maintain morale, avoid wasted resources) even when 311 counts remain high; target where real problems are hidden by 311 undercounts
- **Free DPW from the 311 trap:** help shift from reactive ticket-chasing to strategic, data-driven corridor cleaning.
- **Give infrastructure-owning agencies a view of trends:** track conditions of city-owned assets over time, to guide maintenance and renewal.
- **Reduce fire risk:** flag vegetation health and fire risk trends before they become emergencies.
- **Point enforcement where it matters:** surface hotspots for blocked bike lanes, illegal parking, and bus stop obstructions.
- **Replace infrequent controller’s office snapshots with continuous insight:** provide real-time data to prioritize street and sidewalk improvements.
